Source-held-out compound-gene relation benchmark

Scope: discrimination of annotated relations from matched unlabelled controls, using frozen representations and a fixed-dimensional interaction readout. Source holdout does not require unseen compounds or genes. The two graph definitions overlap and are analysed separately.

Broad retains the collected relation labels. Target-related retains: target, drug_target, dpi, drug_binding_gene, binds_chbg, drug_inhibition_gene, drug_activation_gene, inhibitor, agonist, antagonist, blocker, activator, modulator, positive modulator, negative modulator, allosteric modulator, enzyme, drug_enzyme, transporter, drug_transporter, carrier, drug_carrier. Each graph underwent independent high-degree-node filtering (degree ceiling 150 with iterative removal of the top 1% of nodes). Consequently the processed target-related graph need not be a subset of the processed broad graph.

Test sources: DGIdb, DrugRep, Hetionet, OpenBioLink, PharMeBINet. All held-source positives are excluded from training and validation; training uses remaining annotations in the integrated graph, including sources beyond the five test sources. Maximum positives: 10,000 train, 3,000 validation, 3,000 test. Model/run identifiers: 31, 37, 41. All methods share each stored split. Balanced 1:1 positive/control classes; maximum-flow matching exactly preserves both compound and gene marginals in each split. Controls exclude all known positives in either graph, duplicates and pairs in other splits. Initial controls undergo 30N proposed double-edge swaps preserving compound and gene counts; this is not claimed to sample uniformly over feasible matchings. Exact split arrays and their hashes are included.

Common gene features: 2,560-dimensional ESM2 embeddings (mean and CLS features), averaged across protein entries associated with each gene symbol and L2-normalized. CGP-Align gene-feature controls similarly average encoded ORF/CRISPR entries by symbol and normalize. Compound features: RDKit 217; RDKit-Morgan 2059; Morgan 2048; AtomPair 2048; Avalon 1024; NYAN 64; ChemBERTa 384; MoLFormer 768; CGP-Align 256. +RDKit denotes concatenation before projection.

For compounds and genes separately, median imputation, StandardScaler and whitened randomized PCA are fitted on unique training entities only. Components: 32 main, 64 sensitivity; PCA iterated_power=7, random_state=41. Pair features concatenate both main effects and the complete cross-product (1088 or 4224 dimensions), followed by train-only standardization. LogisticRegression uses C=1, solver=lbfgs, class_weight=balanced, tol=1e-4, max_iter=3000. No hyperparameter selection uses test scores. Every fit converged. Validation scores were retained but were not used to choose C or PCA dimension.

Primary metrics: sklearn average_precision_score (AUPRC) and roc_auc_score. Figure summaries average three repeats within each source, then average sources with equal weight. Error bars in Fig. 4c are sample SD across three repeats. The entity-frequency matching makes entity-degree-only global AP and AUROC exactly 0.5; per-query macro AP has a different finite-sample expectation and is diagnostic only.

Reproduction: download the relation-features group with configs/downloads/benchmark_inputs_20261112.json, then run python reproduction/relation_benchmark/run_interaction.py --features <download directory>/features --output <new results directory> --dimension 32 --workers 1. Use --dimension 64 and a separate output directory for sensitivity analysis. The runner evaluates 13 common-ESM2 methods and two CGP-Align gene-feature controls. --methods and --split optionally select a subset. Plot tables contain per-run and aggregate values; source identifiers are lowercase. strict in machine-readable files denotes target-related relations. See reproduction/README.md for the complete commands and provenance.

Pair indices: first column indexes required_compounds.json[smiles] (indices records the original compound-entity rows); second column indexes gene_symbols.json. Both lists preserve the feature-array order.
